### 3. Core Technical Concepts & Formulas You Must Explain

#### A. Audio Preprocessing Pipeline
1. **Monophonic Downmixing & Normalization:**
   Raw audio sampled at varying rates is downmixed to mono and linearly resampled to $16\text{ kHz}$. Amplitude is peak-normalized:
   $$x[n] = \frac{x_{\text{raw}}[n]}{\max(|x_{\text{raw}}[n]|) + \epsilon}$$
2. **Log-Mel Spectrogram Transformation:**
   * STFT computes $X(m, k)$ with $N_{\text{fft}} = 1024$ ($64\text{ ms}$ window) and $\text{hop} = 512$ ($32\text{ ms}$ step).
   * Energy is mapped onto $64$ Mel-scale triangular filterbanks $M(f, m)$ spanning $0\text{ Hz}$ to $8\text{ kHz}$:
     $$m = 2595 \cdot \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right)$$
   * Log transformation converts power to decibel-like scale:
     $$S(f, t) = \log\left(\text{MelSpectrogram}(x) + 1e-10\right)$$
3. **Patch Masking Strategy ($25\%$ Ratio):**
   A binary mask $M \in \{0, 1\}^{64 \times 320}$ is generated by Bernoulli sampling with $p = 0.25$. Masked bins are zeroed out before passing to the encoder, forcing the network to learn contextual acoustic continuity across temporal and harmonic axes.

#### B. Bounded Conjugate-Symmetric Phase Scrambling
* **Concept:** Physical machine faults change magnitude spectra (friction/clicks), whereas background noise phase shifts randomly. Standard augmentations (pitch shift) ruin physical machine harmonics.
* **Math:** Given Real FFT $X(k) = |X(k)| e^{j \phi(k)}$, we add bounded phase perturbations $\Delta \phi(k) \sim U(-\pi \cdot s, \pi \cdot s)$ with $s = 0.20$:
  $$\tilde{X}(k) = |X(k)| e^{j (\phi(k) + \Delta \phi(k))}$$
* **Symmetry Constraint:** $\Delta \phi(0) = 0$ (DC component) and $\Delta \phi(N/2) = 0$ (Nyquist frequency) are held at zero to guarantee that the inverse STFT produces a strictly real-valued audio waveform.

### 4. Anticipated Viva / Evaluator Questions & High-Scoring Answers

#### Q1: "Why use log-Mel spectrograms instead of raw waveforms or MFCCs?"
> **High-Scoring Answer:**  
> Raw audio waveforms are 1D high-frequency samples ($160,000$ points for $10\text{ seconds}$), making it computationally heavy and difficult for 2D convolutions to extract shift-invariant spatial patterns. MFCCs discard fine harmonic energy distributions by applying a Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to compress channels down to ~13-20 coefficients. Log-Mel spectrograms preserve 2D spatial-temporal structures (vertical harmonic structures across Mel bins and horizontal continuous temporal patterns across frames), matching human auditory perception while enabling 2D CNN feature learning.

#### Q2: "Why is 25% masking ratio chosen for audio MAE instead of 75% like Vision MAE (He et al.)?"
> **High-Scoring Answer:**  
> Natural images have extreme spatial redundancy (e.g., large patches of blue sky or green grass), so Vision MAE requires a $75\%$ mask ratio to prevent trivial copying. Audio log-Mel spectrograms are dense, non-stationary continuous textures where acoustic events (bearing clicks, transient friction) occur over short time intervals and narrow frequency bands. A $75\%$ mask destroys essential acoustic context; empirical tuning confirmed $25\%$ strikes the perfect balance between task difficulty and contextual learning capacity.

#### Q3: "How does phase scrambling differ from adding Gaussian noise?"
> **High-Scoring Answer:**  
> Adding Gaussian noise distorts both the **magnitude spectrum** and the phase spectrum, altering the signal-to-noise ratio and masking quiet mechanical harmonics. Conjugate-symmetric phase scrambling perturbs **only the phase** while preserving $100\%$ of the exact magnitude spectrum $|X(k)|$. This creates acoustic variations that retain true machine energy dynamics, teaching the model to build phase-invariant healthy embeddings.
